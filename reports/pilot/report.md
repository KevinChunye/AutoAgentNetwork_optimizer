# AutoAgentNetwork pilot (protocol v2): on BBEH, the Haiku team beat one Haiku on the gate but not on held-out items, and one Sonnet call matched it

<div class="byline">Pilot sessions 1 to 4, 2026-10-18 to 2026-10-19 (Claude Code, branch <code>pilot_run</code>) · for Kevin Wang · every number below is generated by <code>analysis/</code> scripts from <code>results/</code> and the ledger</div>

<div class="abstract" markdown="1">

**Abstract.** The pilot asks where a multi-agent LLM pipeline's accuracy comes from, and how a fixed budget of agent calls should be spent across stages and across tasks (H1 to H6, protocol v2). The pipeline is a propose → critique → refine → aggregate network of 19 Claude Haiku 5.5 calls. Session 4 had working keys and ran the pilot to its end for ${{value:spent_total_usd}} of the $20 cap (Anthropic ${{value:spent_anthropic_usd}}, OpenAI ${{value:spent_openai_usd}}).

The answer, at pilot scale:

- **The setup needed four changes before it could measure anything.**
  - Haiku 5.5's tokenizer gives about 2.2 characters per token, not 4, so every `max_tokens` cap went from 300–800 to 3200 (Kevin, three decisions).
  - Omni-MATH was too easy for single Haiku: 65–80% on both difficulty windows tried. The gate moved to four BIG-Bench Extra Hard tasks, where single Haiku scores 40%.
  - The math and BBEH graders misread real answer formats, and were fixed with tests before the data they would affect.
- **The gate passed.** On the 60 judged items, dense beat single Haiku by +21.7 points (95% CI +6.7 to +36.7) and the free six-answer vote by +20.0 (+8.3 to +31.7). Critique and refinement, not voting, carried the gain.
- **It did not replicate on the held-out half.** On P4's 40 held-out items, dense beat single Haiku by only +2.5 (−12.5 to +17.5). Pooled over all 100 validation items, the gap is +14 (+3 to +25), and it is biased upward by how the gate was reached.
- **One Sonnet 5.5 call per task matched the 19-call team.** Over 80 items, Sonnet minus dense is +8.75 points (−6.25 to +22.5), at 1.25× dense's cost. A manager should buy the senior.
- **No pruned or consensus design kept dense's accuracy at half its cost (H5 not met).** The search-chosen consensus policy came within 2.5 points but at 75% of dense's cost. The cheapest good static team, 3-3-1, scored 55% at 46% of the cost, below single Haiku.
- **The production function is flat beyond one critic.** Doubling proposers adds +3.1 points (CI +0.3 to +5.8). Doubling critics adds +1.3 and doubling refiners +1.4, both under 2 points as point estimates. Six proposers behave like 1.9 independent ones (H6 holds).

**Recommendation:** do not scale up the pruning study. If Kevin wants the premise settled, the only question worth money is dense against single Haiku against one Sonnet call on about 600 fresh BBEH items: about $16 actual, against a governor worst case of $76 (§7).

## 1. Question and pre-registered hypotheses

**Question (protocol v2).** Where does a multi-agent pipeline's accuracy come from? How should a fixed budget of agent calls be allocated across stages (static allocation) and across tasks (dynamic allocation)? Cost is measured in cache-aware dollars at list price.

- **H1, stages interact:** a joint search over per-stage keep levels beats stage-by-stage sensitivity analysis at matched cost.
- **H2, unequal value:** stages differ in accuracy gained per dollar.
- **H3, label-free reward:** agreement with the dense network picks configs close to those the labeled reward picks.
- **H4, controls (primary outcome):** every gain must beat matched-cost random pruning, the single agent, a free vote over the six proposers and one Sonnet 5.5 call.
- **H5, management by exception:** a consensus policy reaches dense accuracy minus 3 points at no more than half the dense cost, on held-out items (rule 24).
- **H6, diagnostic:** the propose stage's effective team size, n/(1+(n−1)ρ), is at most 3.

The pilot is not powered to test these; it estimates effects with intervals and decides by rules written before each run (`research/plan.md`, amendments 1 to 6, rules 1 to 65). Twenty independent audits ran during session 4 (25 in all), one after every paid run or set of runs; their verdicts are in Appendix D.

## 2. Setup as run

**Network.** `configs/networks/dense_3x6.yaml`: 6 proposers, 6 critics and 6 refiners, each reading every output of the stage before, plus 1 aggregator. That is 19 calls per task. Every stage has a 3200-token cap (§3.1). Pruning keeps the first *k* agents of a stage; the P4 grid uses k ∈ {1, 3, 6} per middle stage (27 configs, 105 distinct calls per task through the call cache).

**Models.** The worker is Claude Haiku 5.5 with thinking off (`haiku_nothink`, chosen in P0). The control and escalation judge is Claude Sonnet 5.5 with thinking off; it rejects `thinking: disabled`, so `sonnet_nothink` sends `thinking: between_tools`, the API's thinking-off setting for that model. GPT-6 Luna was dropped in session 3 (hidden reasoning 3.6–4.1×).

**Task: BIG-Bench Extra Hard, four tasks.** BBEH (Kazemi et al., ACL 2025) items are generated by procedures, so recall cues were absent from all its outputs (0 of 126 banding outputs).

- **Tasks kept** by the pre-registered band rule (`analysis/banding.py`): geometric shapes, web of lies, dyck languages and boolean expressions. Single Haiku scored 20/50 = 40% on their banding pool.
- **Gate file `bbeh_gate`:** 749 items, hash-ordered.
- **Slices:**
  - calibration 0–19;
  - validation 20–59 (P3, P4 search half), 60–99 (P4 held-out half), 100–119 (gate widening);
  - test 120+, untouched.

**Budget and governor.** Lifetime caps are $100 per account; the pilot phase is capped at $20 and each run at $4. Before every call, the governor reserves its worst case (prompt at the cache-write rate plus the full cap of output, ×1.15). Every paid config passed `aan check` (prereg present, file committed, worst case fits).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 P0 calibration: the tokenizer halves the budgets

{{table:p0_calibration}}

- Haiku 5.5 with thinking off wrote about 2.1–2.4 characters per token on this math text, and prompts came to 1.75× the 4-characters-per-token estimate.
- At the original caps (300–800 tokens), the dense network truncated 11 of 12 critiques and 1 of 2 aggregators. In one task, five proposers and all six refiners were right, but the aggregator was cut off before its answer.
- No Haiku ref truncated less, so the pre-registered kill sent the question to Kevin.
  - He doubled every cap, with the aggregate cap at refine's (1600), then raised critique to 1600 as well.
  - After Omni banding still truncated 2 of 10 single calls at 1600, he set a uniform 3200.
- At 3200, no call in the gate runs truncated, and one of about 8,400 P4 grid calls did. Unused tokens cost nothing: actual spend ran at 0.07–0.47× the governor's worst case.

### 3.2 P2: choosing a gate task

- **Omni-MATH was too easy.**
  - Difficulty 4.4–6.1: single Haiku 81/117 = 69% (hand grade 71%).
  - Difficulty 6.1–7.8 (one move up): 12/25 = 48% on its banding pool, but 13/20 = 65% on calibration, above the 60% ceiling under both grades. So Omni failed P2.
  - Many Omni outputs leaned on recall ("this is a known problem"): 39% at 4.4–6.1 and most at 6.1–7.8. Those were less often right.
- **BBEH banding** kept four computational tasks, pooled 40%. Time arithmetic (100%) and sarc triples (0%) fell outside the band.
- **AIME was not banded** (rule 49): it is the most contaminated candidate.
- **BBEH P2b** (calibration 0–19, descriptive under rule 56):
  - single 8/20, dense 18/20 (fixed grader);
  - vote6 9/20; voting added nothing over the average proposer.

### 3.3 P3 and P3w: the gate on fresh items

{{figure:a_gate_p3|Figure a. Gate on the judged items (validation 20–59 and 100–119): accuracy with 95% paired bootstrap CIs.}}

{{table:gate_p3}}

- **Futility check** (rule 19), on validation 20–39: gap +10 (−20 to +35), so not futile.
- **Rule 12 widened the gate.** On 20–59 both CIs included 0, so P3w added items 100–119.
- **On the 60 judged items, rule 11 passes under the automatic grade, the blind hand grade and the fixed grader:**
  - dense 65.0%, single 43.3%: gap **+21.7 (+6.7 to +36.7)**;
  - dense minus vote6 **+20.0 (+8.3 to +31.7)**.
- **Noise:** two independent dense runs agreed on 7 of 10 final answers.
- **The gain comes from checking, not voting.** Stage accuracy of the dense run was:
  - propose 45%
  - critique 58%
  - refine 61%
  - aggregate 63%

  At least one proposer was right on 80–95% of items, but the plurality vote recovered only 45–50%. Critics fixed answers by re-deriving them: on one geometry item no proposer was right, while four critics were.
- **The senior control.** One Sonnet call per task on 20–59 scored 70% (fixed grader) against dense's 65%, at $0.0140 against $0.0111 per task.
  - Rule 18 as amended by rule 58: Sonnet stops P4 only if it is at least as accurate **and** no dearer. It was dearer, so P4 ran, and the report leads with the senior (Kevin).
  - The automatic grade first scored Sonnet at 55%: Sonnet writes web-of-lies answers in angle brackets, the format BBEH's own prompt shows. Kevin approved the grader fix after P3, for both arms; frozen-grader tables are kept beside the fixed ones.

Three readings of the collapse on the held-out half, none of them the headline (audit 25):

## 5. Threats to validity

- **Selection and optional stopping.** The task family, the window and the gate widening were all chosen by rules that react to data. The held-out half is the only estimate free of that, and its n is 40.
- **Small n.** At n = 40, paired CIs are about ±15–20 points; every held-out comparison is a point estimate with a wide interval.
- **Noise.** Dense agreed with itself on 7 of 10 items across two runs. Without a temperature setting, each cell is one sample.
- **Grader changes.**
  - Every change was made with tests, before the data it would affect, or regraded from the cache with frozen and fixed tables side by side (rules 42, 51, 61).
  - Golds never changed (data-card hashes are identical).
  - A blind hand grade backs every gate decision (rule 46).
- **Recall.** BBEH is public; part of Sonnet's lead may be recall, which would help the single arms and the team alike.
- **Prompts.** The stage prompts were written for math. A team tuned for BBEH might do better; nothing here tests that.
- **Latency** numbers mix cached and concurrent calls and are not speedup measurements.

## 7. Recommendation

**Stop the pruning line of work.** On the one task family where Haiku sits at 40%, the 19-call team's edge over one Haiku call did not replicate on held-out items. A single Sonnet call matched or beat it at 1.25× the cost. No pruned or consensus team kept its accuracy at half the cost. The stage-width results (flat beyond one critic and one refiner) are useful, but they do not make compression the question worth funding.

**If Kevin wants the premise settled at power:** the core of option B is dense, single Haiku and one Sonnet call on 600 fresh BBEH items. Positions 120–719 are untouched; that uses the test slice. It has a paired MDE of about 6 points.

- **Cost:** about $16 actual (dense $0.0118, Sonnet $0.0147, single $0.0008 per task, from the pilot). The governor's worst case is $76.22.
- **Free from the same runs:** vote6, every consensus policy and vote-or-Sonnet.
- **Not worth funding:** P4's picks (+$12.29 worst case) and the cascade (+$75.12).

The exact `configs/budget.yaml` change is in `decision_memo.md`.
