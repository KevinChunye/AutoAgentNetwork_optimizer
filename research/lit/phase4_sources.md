## 1. Error floor

### Don't Always Pick the Highest-Performing Model: An Information Theoretic View of LLM Ensemble Selection (arXiv preprint, 2026)
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08003 ; HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08003 ; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08003v1 ; metadata via https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=2602.08003
- What it does: chooses which models to query under a budget by maximising the mutual information between the true label and the selected models' answers (greedy, estimated from data). To explain why adding models stops helping, it models correlated errors with a Gaussian copula (a way of tying together the errors of several models through one shared hidden variable) and proves a non-zero limit on the ensemble's error.
- Identity check: the ID matches. Authors: Yigit Turkmen, Baturalp Buyukates, Melih Bastopcu. Published 2026-02-08T15:05:22Z, v1 only, no comment or journal reference on arXiv. Primary category cs.LG; also cs.AI, cs.DC, cs.IT, stat.ML. The title does not say "error floor"; the floor is a theorem inside (Section 4.3).
- Key numbers (quoted or read):
  - Abstract, in pieces: "Large language models (LLMs) are often ensembled together to improve overall reliability and robustness" / ", but in practice models are strongly correlated." / "to explain why performance can saturate even with many models, we model the correlated errors" / "of the models using Gaussian-copula and show an information-theoretic error floor" / "for the performance of the ensemble." / "We test our approach in two question answering datasets and one binary sentiment classification dataset:" / "MEDMCQA, MMLU, and IMDB movie reviews." / "Across all datasets, we observe that our method consistently outperforms strong baselines" / "under the same query budget."
  - Model, Eq. (1) (one hidden factor U shared by all m models): `Z_j = \sqrt{\rho}\,U + \sqrt{1-\rho}\,\xi_j`. Eq. (2): the error indicator is `E_j = 1(Z_j < \tau_j)` with threshold `\tau_j = \Phi^{-1}(\epsilon_j)`. So rho is the correlation of the hidden Gaussians Z_j.
  - Theorem 4.4 (MAP Information Saturation), setup: uniform pairwise correlation rho > 0 and marginal accuracy alpha = 1 - epsilon > 1/2. Eq. (11), verbatim: `\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\hat{Y}_{\text{MAP}}\neq Y)=\Phi\left(\frac{\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)>0`. Here Phi is the standard normal CDF and Y-hat_MAP is the maximum-a-posteriori (most probable label given all answers) decision.
  - Equality or bound: the digest reported that the theorem text says the error is "lower-bounded by" this expression while Eq. (11) and Appendix C write it as an equality (Appendix C: "which is strictly positive for any rho>0 and alpha in (1/2,1)"). Use it as the limiting value for the stated model.
  - Appendix C, Remark C.1: "the error floor lies in (0,1/2) for any rho>0". Limits: the floor vanishes as rho goes to 0 and approaches 1 - alpha as rho goes to 1. No numeric example is given in the paper.
  - Section 4.3: "as m grows, the error probability converges to a non-zero floor determined by the correlation structure"; "scaling the ensemble size m cannot overcome structural correlations". Section 7: "correlations induce a non-vanishing ensemble error probability".
  - Appendix D, Theorem D.1 ("price of difficulty"): with D a hidden prompt-difficulty variable independent of the label and answers conditionally independent given (label, D), `I(Y;X_S) = I(Y;X_S|D) - I(Y;D|X_S)`; `I(Y;X_S|D)` is monotone submodular in the chosen set S, which gives a (1 - 1/e) guarantee for greedy selection.
  - Experiments: MEDMCQA mean estimated correlation 0.55, "performance approaches a non-zero floor, consistent with the saturation phenomenon" (the paper blames part of the plateau on noise in the MAP estimate, because many answer patterns are rare when k is large). MMLU: methods "eventually converge" at larger k (about 0.171 by k = 13), attributed mainly to the estimator's variance. IMDB: mean correlation 0.90, "improvements from ensemble selection are modest."
  - My arithmetic from Eq. (11) [inference; hand-computed from normal-table values to three digits, recompute with scipy before use], for alpha = 0.7 (single-model error 0.30): rho = 0.25 gives a floor of about 0.147; rho = 0.5 gives about 0.229. Check: Phi^-1(0.3) = -0.5244.
- Relevance to AutoAgentNetwork: **supports** 4C (a published theoretical reason for saturation) and **threatens novelty** for any claim that "an error floor exists": it is published (Feb 2026). What stays open is the measured curve with dollars attached. It is also a **nearby prior** for 4B: budgeted choice of which models to query by an information criterion, evaluated with a query budget (not dollars).
- Two mismatches with the plan's text [inference from the equations above]:
  1. The plan's rho (0.48 for Haiku repeats) is a correlation between 0/1 error indicators. The paper's rho is the correlation of the hidden Gaussians behind those indicators. Turning a Gaussian correlation into 0/1 indicators lowers it (the tetrachoric versus phi relationship), so the hidden rho that matches an observed indicator correlation of 0.48 is larger. Plugging 0.48 into Eq. (11) would put the floor too low. Estimate the hidden rho by fitting, or report the empirical curve only.
  2. The plan's `n_eff = m / (1 + (m - 1) rho)` is a variance formula for averaging equicorrelated scores. Eq. (11) is the error rate of a decision rule under a different model. They share the idea "correlation caps the gain" but not a formula, so cite them separately.
- Resources: no code or data link in the text I read (both halves of the HTML were read; the digest said it could not rule out a link in a truncated tail). The paper names OpenRouter for model access. Paper licence not checked.
- Open questions it raises: does the floor apply to repeated calls of one model (exchangeable errors, which the one-factor model covers algebraically) as well as to different models? What fraction of the plateau in a 100-call curve is hidden difficulty and what fraction is estimator noise?
